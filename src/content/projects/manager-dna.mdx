## The Core Question: Alpha or Disguised Risk?

In active management, the most critical question is whether a fund manager is generating genuine alpha or simply taking on disguised factor risk. "Manager DNA" is a quantitative research pipeline I built to reverse-engineer the behavioral tendencies of active ETF managers.

## Beyond the Static Grid

Traditional fund analysis relies heavily on static models, like the standard Morningstar 3x3 style box. The problem? Markets aren't static. A static grid fails to capture how a manager adapts when the market is under stress. I wanted to build a tool that explicitly detects "style drift"—revealing whether a manager holds firm to their mandate during a downturn or tactically rotates their exposures.

## The Quantitative Engine

To track this behavior dynamically, I designed a three-stage regime-aware pipeline:

- **Factor Extraction:** The system first extracts active return factor loadings using a rolling 63-day OLS regression against the Fama-French 5-Factor model.
- **Market Regime Classification:** Rather than using arbitrary cutoffs, I applied Gaussian Mixture Modeling (GMM) over macroeconomic indicators (SPY returns, VIX, credit spreads, 10Y yields) to provide a soft, probabilistic classification of current market states.
- **Mapping the DNA:** I used Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to extract orthogonal 'Super-Styles' from the beta matrix. By layering cosine similarity networks over the GMM-defined market regimes, the pipeline constructs a bipartite conviction network.

## The Edge

By calculating Louvain communities and spectral distances across these multi-layer graphs, the tool quantifies style drift at both the intra-fund and universe levels. It applies rigorous econometrics and unsupervised learning to noisy financial data to help separate true conviction from cyclical luck.
