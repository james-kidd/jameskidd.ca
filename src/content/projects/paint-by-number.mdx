## From Pixels to Futures

In commodity markets, accurate supply forecasting often comes down to tracking the physical maturation of crops from space. This project serves as the groundwork for an automated, deterministic computer vision pipeline designed to track vegetation progressions (from healthy green, to yellowing, to harvest-ready brown) across satellite imagery to predict agricultural yields.

## The "Paint-by-Number" Sandbox

To refine the segmentation logic in a controlled environment, I currently expose the core engine as an interactive "Paint-by-Number" web application. It takes complex visual data and cleanly reduces it into discrete, contiguous regions based on color and spatial proximity.

## The Algorithmic Architecture

While the industry default is often to throw deep learning at image problems, I explicitly avoided black-box neural networks in favor of classical, deterministic methods. When dealing with massive time-series multispectral agricultural data, interpretability, strict reproducibility, and scale matter most.

- **Perceptual Uniformity:** The pipeline operates entirely in the CIELAB color space, ensuring that mathematical distances between pixels perfectly match human-perceived color differences.
- **Superpixel Clustering:** I used Simple Linear Iterative Clustering (SLIC) in a joint 5D LAB+XY space, allowing precise control over the trade-off between color adherence and shape regularity.
- **Palette Reduction:** The system extracts region mean colors into k-means clustering to define a representative palette, applying a Region Adjacency Graph (RAG) to seamlessly collapse similar adjacent superpixels.

## The Edge

This architecture guarantees highly efficient $O(N)$ complexity. It demonstrates that the best solution for commercial data processing isn't always the trendiest AI model, but rather a mathematically sound, highly scalable deterministic pipeline.
